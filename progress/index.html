Every figure here is derived from the monthly prices in the site's own data file, using the rules published on the Strategy page. Where a month is marked ≠, what I actually bought differed from what the rules called for, and both numbers are shown. Figures in US dollars and they include the $3 brokerage on every buy, which is taken from the cash after the rules have set the share count. They ignore FX conversion costs, tax and dividends, so the real return is still a little worse than what you see here. TQQQ's 0.86% management fee is not subtracted here, because a fund charges it inside its own price rather than billing you, so these figures already carry it. Subtracting it again would count it twice. The estimate below shows roughly what it has cost, and there is a fuller explanation on the home page. It cuts the other way too: the cash reserve is credited with interest at 4% a year, accrued monthly, which is roughly what a high-interest saver or offset account pays. That interest counts as return, not as money in, and the figure below shows how much of the gain came from it.
